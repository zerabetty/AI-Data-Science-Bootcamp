# Customer Segmentation with Unsupervised Learning

## Project Overview

The aim of this project is to segment customers based on their purchasing behavior using unsupervised machine learning techniques.

Customer behavior was represented using features such as customer tenure, recency, purchase frequency, and total customer value.

Two clustering approaches were applied and compared:

- K-Means Clustering
- Hierarchical Clustering

The resulting customer groups were analyzed and translated into meaningful business segments.

---

## Dataset

The dataset contains approximately 20,000 customer records and includes information about:

- Customer registration dates
- Online and offline purchase behavior
- Order frequency
- Customer spending
- Most recent purchase dates

---

## Data Preparation

The following preprocessing steps were performed:

- Dataset structure and descriptive statistics were examined.
- Customer behavior variables were created.
- Extreme values were analyzed.
- Numerical variables were standardized before clustering.

The main features used for customer segmentation were:

- `TENURE`
- `RECENCY`
- `TOTAL_ORDER_NUM`
- `TOTAL_CUSTOMER_VALUE`

---

## Feature Engineering

Several customer-level behavioral features were created.

### TENURE

Represents how long the customer has been associated with the company.

### RECENCY

Represents the number of days since the customer's most recent purchase.

### TOTAL_ORDER_NUM

Represents the total number of online and offline purchases.

### TOTAL_CUSTOMER_VALUE

Represents the total monetary value generated by the customer.

---

## K-Means Clustering

The optimal number of clusters was investigated using the **Elbow Method**.

Based on the analysis, the customers were divided into **5 clusters** using K-Means.

The clusters were analyzed according to their behavioral characteristics and converted into meaningful business segments.

### Customer Segments

| Segment | Description |
|---|---|
| High Value Loyal | Highly valuable customers with very frequent purchases |
| Loyal Active | Active customers with relatively high purchasing activity |
| Long-Term Slipping | Long-term customers whose purchasing activity has declined |
| Recent Low-Frequency | Recently active customers with relatively low purchase frequency |
| At Risk | Customers with low activity and high recency |

---

## Hierarchical Clustering

Hierarchical Clustering was also applied to evaluate whether a different clustering approach would identify similar customer structures.

A dendrogram was generated to visualize the relationships between customer groups.

The hierarchical clustering solution was also divided into five major customer segments.

---

## Model Comparison

The customer segments generated by K-Means and Hierarchical Clustering were compared.

The segment agreement rate between the two approaches was:

**82.43%**

This relatively high agreement indicates that both algorithms identified similar underlying customer behavior patterns.

## Business Insights

The segmentation results can support different customer relationship strategies:

- **High Value Loyal:** Retention, loyalty programs, and personalized premium offers.
- **Loyal Active:** Cross-selling and loyalty-building campaigns.
- **Long-Term Slipping:** Reactivation strategies before customer engagement declines further.
- **Recent Low-Frequency:** Campaigns designed to increase purchase frequency.
- **At Risk:** Win-back and reactivation campaigns.

## Conclusion

Both K-Means and Hierarchical Clustering identified similar customer behavior structures.

The **82.43% segment agreement rate** suggests that the resulting segmentation is relatively robust across different clustering approaches.

K-Means provides a simple and scalable solution for operational customer segmentation, while Hierarchical Clustering provides additional insight into the relationships between customer groups.

The resulting segments can be used to support data-driven marketing, customer retention, and reactivation strategies.
